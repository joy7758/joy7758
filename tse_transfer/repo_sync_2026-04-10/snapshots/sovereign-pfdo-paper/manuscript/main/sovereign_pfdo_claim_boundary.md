## Core Question

How can protocol-layer mechanisms support distributed governance and knowledge
sovereignty in machine-actionable data environments?

## Allowed Main Claims

- Governance-sensitive data systems require architectural attention at the
  protocol layer rather than only at external policy layers.
- A layered sovereign-governance architecture can be specified in a coherent
  way for distributed object handling, gateway mediation, and intervention.
- A BLAKE3-based PID construction template can be proposed as a bounded design
  mechanism for identity binding and object handling under distributed
  conditions.
- A divergence-triggered gateway intervention model can be proposed as a
  bounded governance-control mechanism for AI-mediated or machine-mediated
  flows.
- Hardware-conscious realization is relevant to the architecture because
  governance mechanisms must coexist with real network-scale constraints.

## Explicit Non-Claims

- This manuscript does not present a finished sovereign infrastructure.
- It does not claim that PID construction alone proves sovereignty,
  authenticity, or legal enforceability.
- It does not claim that KL divergence is a complete theory of AI alignment or
  governance correctness.
- It does not claim that 1.6T-scale or similar throughput targets have already
  been fully demonstrated for the entire framework.
- It does not claim exhaustive protocol standardization, deployment readiness,
  or comprehensive interoperability validation.

## Separation From Other Paper Lines

This manuscript is not a minimal implementation witness paper. It is not
organized around a profile, validator, controlled examples, or runnable demo as
its primary contribution center.

### Not The TSE Line

This manuscript is not a verification-boundary paper. It does not center its
argument on operation accountability as a first-class verification boundary,
same-case comparison, or failure-taxonomy framing.
