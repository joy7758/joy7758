Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript titled "Operation Accountability as a
First-Class Verification Boundary for Machine-Actionable Object Systems" for
consideration as a regular research submission.

This manuscript addresses a deliberately narrow software engineering question:
whether one agent operation can be expressed as an independently checkable
accountability artifact with a bounded verification path. The submission uses
`Execution Evidence and Operation Accountability Profile v0.1`, a fixed-shape
operation accountability statement, a profile-aware validator exposed through
`agent-evidence validate-profile <file>`, a controlled valid and invalid
example set, and one runnable metadata-enrichment demo as bounded supporting
evidence for a verification-boundary argument. The paper keeps its claims
conservative. It does not present a general governance platform, a deployment
study, or broad external-validity claims beyond the documented artifact
boundary.

I also disclose a related JSS witness manuscript from the same broader
research agenda. That JSS line was the witness-oriented minimal implementation
manuscript and is no longer under review or otherwise active as a submission
line. In contrast, the present TSE submission is the flagship
problem-defining paper centered on operation accountability as a first-class
verification boundary, with a failure taxonomy, same-case comparison, and an
evidence-bounded validation framing. The artifact components in the TSE
submission serve only as bounded supporting evidence for that argument.

I believe the manuscript may fit TSE because it frames a concrete software
artifact as a bounded verification mechanism and studies how a reviewable
research object can support independent checking without overstating broader
system claims.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,

Bin Zhang
